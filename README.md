Smart water meters (SWMs) have transitioned from coarse billing instruments into fine-resolution temporal data-logging devices. This fine resolution data stream creates novel opportunities for disaggregating water consumption into discrete, fixture-level end-use events, implementing residential water anomaly detection, and non-intrusive behavioral monitoring due to the specificity of duration, timing, and flow signatures. However, extracting operational intelligence from the data presents a fundamental informatics challenge: socio-technical signal ambiguity. Because most SWMs measure an aggregated, one-dimensional flow rate over time, distinct water infrastructure failures and anomalous human-induced events coincide with identical time-series signatures creating a masked signal with indistinguishable patterns.
To address this ambiguity, we propose a multiclass anomaly taxonomy and systematic detection framework that decouples behavioral consumer usage from infrastructural anomalies using fine-resolution residential smart water meter data collected over 2 years from a single-family home in the Midwestern United States. We derive the taxonomy by synthesizing the causal duality of residential water-use anomalies and three features encoded in a smart water meter signal (duration, magnitude, and frequency). Our approach resolves duration ambiguity by a fixed threshold on elapsed flow time and frequency ambiguity by testing event-cluster timing regularity. The detection architecture operationalizes this taxonomy through a calibration stage integrating Extreme Value Theory to model per-fixture duration thresholds, statistical process control to detect low-magnitude continuous background flow during expected no-flow periods, and adaptive moving baselines to identify short to medium-term deviations from routine. This approach demonstrates consumer-induced and infrastructural anomaly detection for additional support through human-in-the-loop verification.
